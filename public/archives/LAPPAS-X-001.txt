LOCKDOWN
A 20-day survival game about cooking for a family in lockdown

Shuhang Chen · Solo project · Unity · C# · 2026

LockDown is a survival game set in one apartment during a 20-day city lockdown. Each morning a supply package arrives, and by Day 7 it may be just noodles, a cabbage and rice. Cooking is a tangram puzzle: you cut the ingredients, fit them into a pan that is too small, and store the leftovers in a fridge where food rots. The family and the neighbours reach you through the phone. Based on my family's lockdown in Shanghai in 2022.

- Twelve ingredients, each a shape of 3 to 7 cells, are cut and fitted into a pan of 18 to 25 cells.
- The fridge is a 6 by 8 grid. From Day 8, food stored for two days starts to rot, and the rot spreads to neighbouring cells.
- The phone has a newspaper, a building group chat and a family chat. On Day 4 the building asks about medicine, on Day 5 the paper reports refills running late, and the same morning Grandpa's box is empty.

Where it started

In 2022 my family was locked down in Shanghai. There were five of us in the apartment: my grandparents, my parents and me. We ate what came in the government supply package, and some of the food had already gone bad when it reached us. My grandparents needed their medicine, and nobody was allowed out to buy it.

My parents kept the house running like that for months. The hardest thing was that nobody could tell us when it would end.

One day

I want the player to feel how boring the lockdown was, so they do almost the same things every day. The mechanics that come in later are only small changes to what is already there, and the main thing the player does is still cooking the meal.

The first version

I finished the first version in July. It ran for thirty days, and anything that had been in the fridge for three days simply disappeared. That was too simple. The player only had to remember which day the food went in.

Now rot starts in one cell of an ingredient and spreads to the cells next to it, and it can move into other food that is touching it. The player has to look at the fridge and decide what to cook first and what to move away.

I also cut the run from thirty days to twenty, and I rebuilt the room and the interface. The pictures show how the game looked in September, before that.

The pan

The cooking puzzle comes from tangram, the Chinese puzzle where flat pieces have to fit inside an outline. In LockDown the pieces are ingredients and the outline is the pan.

A tangram has one correct answer. I didn't want that here, because there were no perfect meals during the lockdown. So every ingredient has a shape, calories and a food type, and there is never room for all of them. So the player has to choose which food to cook today and which to leave.

The knife

On Day 1 the handle comes off the family's knife, and for five days every ingredient goes into the pan whole. On Day 6 a new knife is at the bottom of the food bag. I use cutting to encourage the player to fit awkwardly shaped ingredients into pans that are a different shape every day.

The puzzle is my own version of tangram, the traditional Chinese puzzle. When I was thinking about how to add variations to it, I thought about what I really do when I cook: I cut food up, and I store some of it for later. Adding those brings my own life into the game. It also gives the player harder puzzles, and makes them think more about how to manage their food.

The fridge

I made the fridge because during the lockdown we had to plan carefully too. We put some of our food away in the fridge to eat later, because we couldn't be sure what was coming. In the game the fridge is broken on Day 1, and Grandpa gets it working again on Day 3. It is a grid like the pan, and the leftovers have to fit in it.

In the game I made this harsher than it was for us. From Day 8 the building loses power at times, and the food in the fridge starts to rot. Rot spreads between cells that touch, and if rotten food goes into the pan, the family loses health. So every morning the player opens the fridge, cooks the oldest things first and drags the bad pieces away from the good ones.

The family and the building

There were five of us at home in 2022. In the game the family has three members, one for each age. Grandpa is old, and his health depends on medicine that has to come from outside. Daughter is young and says it out loud when she is hungry, bored or scared. The player is in the middle and does the cooking.

The neighbors are in the building's group chat, which comes from the residents' group our compound had. For it I picked four kinds of people who were typical of the lockdown. Director Zhang is the official one, who posts the notices. Aunt Li is the helpful one, who shares what she has. Mr. Wang is the pessimist, who doubts the notices and argues with them. The stranger on the sixth floor is the one who profits from the shortage, with medicine to trade for food.

The phone

In 2022 the news, our residents' group and my own family would all talk about the same shortage, and each of them said something different. So the phone in the game has the same three places to read: a newspaper, the building's group chat and the family chat.

Day 5 is an example. The newspaper says prescriptions are late all over the city. In the building group Aunt Li looks for her own box of tablets and finds it empty. In the family chat Grandpa says his are gone.

The newspaper never says when the lockdown will end. On Day 11 it reports that a reopening notice going around the chats was false.

Choices

Five of the twenty days have a choice. It comes as a chat message, and the player answers in the chat. Whichever answer the player picks, the family loses something.

On Day 5 Grandpa's tablets are gone. The player can ask the neighbors, which is free but may not work. They can send Director Zhang a photo of the empty box and wait for a pharmacy van. Or they can give two things from the fridge to the stranger on the sixth floor and get the medicine now.

The later choices are about food. A sealed floor asks for some, and Grandpa wants to give his share away.

The last morning

The lockdown ends after Day 20. Nothing in the game says it is coming, and the last day starts like all the others. Then the report comes up. It shows how many days the family spent at home and how healthy everyone is.

If anyone's health reaches zero, the run stops on that day and the same report comes up. I kept the report this short because I don't want the ending to feel like winning.

How to play

Left mouse: click the floor to walk, click the package, the counter or the fridge to use it, drag food into the pan or the fridge.

Q and E: turn the camera. Tab: phone. R: rotate the piece. Right mouse: put a piece back, or throw food out of the fridge. Space: make the cut. Esc: close.

Sniff & Snitch
A two-player online stealth game

Shuhang Chen · Programmer, team project · Unity 6 · C# · Unity Relay · 2026 · In development

Two players sneak through a guarded prison online, one as Lyra, a tall and strong human, and one as Fia, a small fox who can climb. Most doors, boxes and guards need both of them. When a guard catches one, he locks them in a gas cage, and only the partner can get them out. This was a team project, and I was a programmer on it.

- The host decides anything both players can see: pickups, doors, cages and guards. Each player still moves their own body on their own computer.
- Only sight starts a chase. A sound can send a guard to look, so the player is never caught by something they couldn't see coming.
- A caught player waits in a gas cage for fifteen seconds, and the partner has to reach it and hold E for a second and a half.

Where it started

Our team started with a plain co-op stealth game, where two players sneak through the same level side by side.

The two players hardly needed each other. Each of them could get past a guard alone, so the second player was mostly company.

I wanted them to interact, so I suggested we do what It Takes Two does: give each character different abilities and make those abilities work on each other. The stealth itself comes from Dishonored 2, where a guard's eyes and ears are rules the player can learn.

Two bodies

Lyra and Fia share the same controls, but their bodies are different, so the same level is a different place for each of them.

Lyra weighs 65 kg and is 1.8 m tall. She can't climb ledges, but she can drag crates and carts and wear a mask. While the mask is on, guards don't see her at all. Fia weighs 15 kg and is 1 m tall. She can dash, jump higher, climb ledges, hide inside crates and drop a rope from a ledge for Lyra.

Many levers can only be pulled by one of them, and many routes only one of them can take. So a room often starts with one player opening the way for the other.

Two pairs of hands

Some things in the level only move when both players work on them at the same time.

- Lift door. Each player grips one side of a heavy door. Once both are holding it, they lift it together and walk under, and it slams shut behind them for good, so neither one can go back alone.

- Lever pairs. In the reservoir the water only rises when two levers are down. One of them only Lyra can pull, and the other only Fia can pull.

- Takedown. A disguised Lyra marks a guard, which leaves him weak for a moment. Fia then drops on him from above and knocks him out.

Who decides

In an online game two computers can disagree about what happened. If both players grab the last iceball at the same moment, each computer may think it got it. So I made the host the only one who decides anything both players can see.

Each player still moves their own body on their own computer, so moving never feels late. But a pickup, a door, a cage or a guard's state is a request to the host, and the host's answer is what both players see.

- Picking a character. Nobody spawns when they connect. Each player claims Lyra or Fia, and the host spawns both once both are taken.

- Two backpacks. Each player has a backpack of their own with four slots. The host changes what is in them, and only the owner sees their slots on screen.

- Guards. All guard AI runs on the host. The other computer only shows where each guard is and how alert he is.

- Checkpoints. A checkpoint saves only once both players have reached it, and only the host writes the save file.

- Offline testing. A designer can start the level from any spot without a network. That path uses a local backpack, and it is never the one a connected player uses.

Seen and heard

A guard sees in a cone and checks the player's head first, then the body, so Fia can hide behind something only a meter high. Lyra needs something a little taller.

He also hears. Sneaking is quiet, walking is louder and running can be heard from far away. Throwing a pebble makes a guard turn his head, and a bottle makes him walk over to look. A wall between the sound and the guard makes it carry less far.

The bolt over his head shows what he knows. Yellow means he heard something, and it can send him to look, but a sound alone never starts a chase. Red means he has seen the player. I made only sight start a chase so that the player is never caught by something they couldn't see coming.

Getting caught

The partner has to reach the cage and hold E for a second and a half to open it. If nobody comes, the level goes back to the last checkpoint.

The cage was my idea. I wanted getting caught to be a story the two players can tell afterwards. The escort is slow and the gas takes time, so the free player always has a chance to come running.

Gadgets

Gadgets go in the backpack and take a slot on the hotbar. Each one belongs to one character. The snowball, the iceball, levitation and the masks are Lyra's. The rope and the pop-up crate are Fia's. If a gadget is dropped, it lies on the floor as a small light, and only the right character can pick it up.

The snowball can be thrown at a guard's feet. It lands as a patch of ice that grows and then melts. Levitation lifts a guard off the ground, and he panics until he is put down. If he was walking a player to a cage, he lets go. A mask changes Lyra's clothes, and while she wears it the mask's own gadgets take over her hotbar.

Pebbles and bottles come from piles that never run out, so a bad throw never leaves the players stuck.

Ice and guards

Gadgets don't have special code for each guard. Things in the level have states such as frozen or burning, and a small table says what each state does to a guard. A frozen floor makes him slip.

So a snowball thrown in the right place stops a chase. The guard runs onto the ice, falls on his back and lies there for a few seconds, and the players get time to hide. It also works on a guard that is only walking his patrol.

The hand and the Spring Gun

I built Lyra's hand and Fia's Spring Gun together, because each one is mostly there for the other. Johnny is building a level around the two of them.

The reservoir

The reservoir is the end of the first level. Eric built the basin and its four water levels. I rebuilt the inside of its three rooms and made the boat the only way across.

I took the idea from the Water Temple in Ocarina of Time: one room for each water level. Each time the players pull a pair of levers, the water rises one stage and the boat can reach the next room.

On the water the work is still split. When Fia rows, Lyra can stand at the bow and throw iceballs at the towers.

Level Viewer

When two of us worked on the same level at once, keeping our changes apart was tiring, and in meetings it was hard to say what we meant while everyone watched one person's Unity screen. Eric had started a web page that shows a level, and I made it into one the whole team can mark up together, live.

- What it shows. The blockout, Lyra's path in green and Fia's in orange, and every guard with his view cone and patrol.

- Marking it up. Anyone can tag a spot where a player waits for their partner, a stretch in a guard's view, or leave a note. Everyone sees it live.

- Back into Unity. A prop placed in the browser arrives in Unity as a ghost. The level designer checks it in place, and an admin approves it before it becomes real.

How to play

W, A, S and D walk, Shift runs and C sneaks. Space jumps, and a tap of Shift is Fia's dash. E uses, lifts and opens a cage. Hold Q for Lyra's masks or Fia's actions, and H drops Lyra's hand.

Left mouse uses a gadget, and the mouse wheel button pings a spot for your partner. V shows your partner's view, and Esc pauses.

Last Mile
A management game about a food-delivery station

Shuhang Chen · Solo project · Unity · C# · 2026

You manage Lanting, a small food-delivery station, for ten days. Orders appear on a 3D map of the district as countdown pins, and you assign each one to a rider, a ground robot or a drone. Riders are paid per drop, and each of them needs money for something by a certain day. From Day 3 headquarters starts adding machines, and each one takes a rider's place on the roster.

- Every order is assigned by hand. The deadline is set from the fastest available courier, so a late order is usually the result of a dispatch decision.
- Four riders each have one bill due on a set day, and an even share of the orders is just enough to cover it.
- The district is a road graph based on Xujiahui, filled in by editor scripts. A headless test plays all ten days and checks that all 132 orders resolve.

The orders

The riders are paid by the drop, so sending Chen instead of Xiaoyu on an ID check decides which of them gets closer to the bill they are saving for. Over the ten days headquarters moves the station onto robots and drones.

The first version was a policy game with sliders for pay and late fees, and a pay cut was spread over everyone, so nobody in particular lost anything. I changed it so the player hands out every order by hand to riders with names.

- The board counts parcels, so a crate counts the same as a coffee.

- Each machine fixes one problem and leaves another.

- AO-6, the assistant from day 6, never overrules the player.

A day

The station gets ¥180 each morning. The player can save it, or spend ¥80 to coach the rider who knows the streets least, ¥90 on breakfast and a rest, or ¥50 to add 15 seconds to the first four orders.

A shift is two 90-second waves. On day 1 six orders arrive one at a time. From day 2 each wave opens with two orders and the rest land in a burst from 36 seconds in, while the first riders are still out, so someone has to wait. Orders rise to 16 a day by day 7. Between waves the player can pay ¥40 for 20 more seconds on new orders, or take ¥60 of rush money that cuts 15 seconds and adds one to the target.

Each rider costs ¥30 in wages at night. The report puts delivered against target next to what each rider took home, so a good day for the station and a bad day for one rider sit on the same page. A station that can't pay wages closes, and the player replays that morning.

Dispatch

Clicking a pin lists who could take the order, with an arrival estimate. A card for someone who can't go is locked and says why, and the clock holds until the player chooses.

The deadline comes from the fastest rested courier on the ground who could take the order, times about 1.4 on day 2 and 1.2 by day 10. I based it on the best courier so that a slower pick, or an order left waiting, is what makes it late. Orders cancel 25 seconds after the deadline.

Each drop costs a rider 8 stamina on top of the ride, and a tired rider slows to 70% speed. Rest gives 12 seconds off and about 36 stamina. Waiting at the station gives nothing, so a break is something the manager has to give.

- Standard: anyone. ¥6 to the station, ¥4 to the rider.

- ID check: a rider, or from day 8 a robot with an ¥80 scanner. ¥8 and ¥5.

- Sixth floor, no lift: a rider or a drone. ¥8 and ¥5.

- Heavy restock: Strength 8. ¥10 and ¥6. A late drop halves both.

The machines

The robot arrives on day 3. It is never paid per drop, but it can't climb stairs or check an ID. That night headquarters compares it with the riders on standard orders. If the riders were on time more often, the player can send it back. A tie goes to the robot.

The drone comes on day 4. It flies straight over the blocks and is the fastest courier on the map, but it carries a load of 5 at most. If the robot stayed, headquarters wants 2 machines on the roster from day 6, 3 from day 8 and 4 on day 10, out of six places, so every machine takes a rider's place.

From day 6 AO-6 recommends someone for every order. Each Confirm, or each time the player picks someone else, is saved as an example. It keeps the last 96 and cites them: "This follows what you did on Day 6, #1058." I wanted the player to train it willingly, one Confirm at a time.

The riders

Each rider has three scores out of 10: Streets for speed, Strength for load, Judgment for tricky handovers. Chen is 9, 4 and 9. Ajie is 5, 9 and 5. Lin is 8, 6 and 6. Xiaoyu joins on day 2 at 4, 6 and 8.

Each is short of money for one thing by one evening: Xiaoyu ¥30 for a room deposit by day 4, Lin ¥40 rent by day 5, Ajie ¥50 he owes a friend by day 6, Chen ¥80 for his wife's medicine by day 9. A fair share of the work just covers it. On the evening her deposit is due, Xiaoyu asks for an order and stops halfway: "Could I... sorry. Could I take this one?" If she falls short she loses the room and starts every shift 20 seconds late.

From day 2 one rider calls each evening about something the board doesn't count. Ajie asks for small parcels, because "the board counts parcels, not weight." I kept one rule for the writing: the riders talk about crates, rent and doorbells, and only AO-6 and headquarters carry the satire.

The district

The map is modelled on Xujiahui: 26 hand-placed junctions around a six-armed crossing, with eight outer districts generated as five-by-five street grids. Orders go to four of them, among them Willow Bend and Heron Road.

An editor script finds each closed block in the road graph, lines its edges with lots and fills the middle with a grid turned to the longest side. The ordinary buildings are built in code. The landmarks and vehicles are AI-generated models that editor scripts fit into the blocks.

The light follows the shift from warm morning to blue evening, but the interface keeps its colours, so a red countdown at dusk looks the same as one at breakfast.

Teaching and testing

Day 1 teaches by doing. Zhou Heng, the regional manager, walks the player through the first dispatch, and later lessons appear the first time they are needed, such as Strength on the first full crate.

The editor can also play the trial by itself. A scripted manager runs all ten days on the real roads and traffic, and the run fails if any of the 132 orders doesn't end in a delivery or a cancellation, if traffic stops, if two bodies overlap, or if a story call is left open.

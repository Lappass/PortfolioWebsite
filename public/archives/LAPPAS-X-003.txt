Trail Adventure
A first-person climbing and wildlife photography game

Shuhang Chen · Two-person project · Unity · C# · 2026

You inherit a cabin and a camera from Leo, an old photographer, on a mountain island with 24 animal species. Each trip starts by packing four tools at the cabin bench and ends at the summit, or when hunger, cold and tiredness have eaten the stamina bar. Any slope over 50 degrees can be climbed. Sixteen islanders ask for photos and send tools back by paper parachute.

- Climbing, running and jumping all spend one stamina bar. Hunger, cold, tiredness, injury and pack weight each shorten it.
- A photo scores one to three stars from what is in the frame: distance, eye contact, flight, young, golden hour. Each species has a list of moments to collect.
- The island, cave and mine are built by editor scripts, and the UI is drawn in code to look like a paper journal.

Where it started

I started hiking after I moved to the United States for university, and I usually had my camera with me. The game comes from those trips. Mount Rainier is the one I remember best. A ground squirrel sat on the rocks and looked straight at me. Higher up it was foggy, and a marmot was watching from a ridge. Both of them lived a long way up, and I only saw them because I had walked that far.

The basic rules of the game are taken from those trips. Animals stay away from the path, so the player has to climb to get to them. Climbing uses stamina. Animals leave once they notice the player, so a good photo usually takes patience.

A few of my photos are in the game too. They are the first pages of Leo's Journal.

The first version

In the first version, the player followed a fixed trail with obstacles on it and rolled a die at each one to get past. I got this idea from real hiking, because on a real mountain you almost always stay on the trail. In the game, though, there was only walking between the obstacles, and it was boring.

While I was testing it, the part I enjoyed most was running around and taking photos of the animals. It also reminded me of Antelope Canyon in Arizona. When I was there I really wanted to leave the trail and walk out into the desert around it. So I decided to build the game around photography and let the player walk wherever they want.

I removed the trails and the dice. Now the player can climb anywhere on the mountain to look for animals, and the mountain is the obstacle.

Climbing and stamina

Climbing is slow on purpose, about 0.6 meters a second. The player goes up one pull at a time, following the hands. I kept the hands low on the screen so they don't cover the rock or an animal sitting on it.

Running, climbing and jumping all use stamina, but walking is free, so that the player can look at the mountain and not at the bar.

Hunger, cold, injury, tiredness and a heavy bag each take a piece off the end of the bar, and each piece has its own color. I like it because I don't have to explain several systems to the player. The stamina bar is the only resource they have to manage, and every other effect is added on top of it. It is also how a real hike works, where being hungry or cold just means you have less energy to climb, and I think that helps the player feel they are really on the mountain.

Photography

In real life, animals get scared when a person shows up. But when they haven't noticed anyone, they carry on with their own things, and every animal has moments of its own that are worth a photo. The scoring follows this. A photo gets more points if the animal is close, if it is running or flying, or if it is looking into the lens.

When I take photos while running, they come out shaky. I also didn't want the player to run up to an animal and take a quick shot, so moving while shooting blurs the photo. A blurred photo only gets half the score and can't be sent for a request.

New photos earn stars, and the player spends the stars at the bench in the cabin to upgrade the camera. I wanted photography to have a positive feedback loop. The more good photos the player takes, the better the camera gets, and with a better camera they can take more and better photos.

Animals

The animals I met on real trips did not all react to me the same way. On Mount Rainier the ground squirrel sat and looked right at me, and the marmot kept watching from a ridge. So in the game all 24 species behave differently. When an animal notices the player, it might stare, freeze, ignore her or walk closer. When it gets scared, it might run, crouch, climb a tree or charge at her.

Some have habits of their own. Foxes steal snacks. Goats spar with each other and will butt anyone who gets too close. The ptarmigan hen won't leave her nest, and the owl is only out from early evening until morning. Sneaking lets the player get nearer before being noticed.

Each animal in the field guide has a list of moments to photograph, such as a close-up, on the run, or at night. Every new moment gives one star, so the player has a reason to go back to an animal they have already photographed.

Legendary creatures

When people are out in nature, they often feel that something like a mountain spirit might live there. I wanted the game to have that too, so the island has nine legendary creatures, taken from Chinese ink landscape paintings and old Chinese stories. They are very hard to find and hard to photograph well. Meeting one is a surprise, and it gives the player one more reason to keep climbing.

Six of them are inside paintings. The player touches a scroll, ink covers the screen, and when it clears she is standing inside the painting. Most of them come out of the mist to watch her, and go back into it if she gets too close.

The other three are in the sky. The Wind Whale circles the island over the sea all day. The Thunder Wyvern only appears after it starts raining, so the player has to pick a day with rain in the forecast.

Requests

Sixteen people live on the island. Each one has a short story of two or three photo requests. The stories don't depend on each other, so getting stuck on a hard request only stops that one story. I spread the requests so that every one of the 24 species is asked for at least once, and this takes the player all over the island.

Every request gives an item back, and all eight tools come from requests. So every tool the player gets has a small story behind it, and it doesn't feel like something she just picked up off the ground.

The Flashlight is an example. The cave is completely dark inside. Nell stands at the cave mouth and won't go in. When the player talks to her, she says that someone else on the island, Shepherd Bram, has a flashlight. The player goes to find Bram and takes a few photos for him, and then he gives her the Flashlight. With it she can go back and explore the whole cave.

The cabin

When I get home from a real trip, I have photos and a few small things from the way. The cabin is where the game keeps those. Every trip starts and ends here.

The mail-order catalogue sells furniture and carvings for stars, so the camera is not the only thing stars can buy.

- Camera bench. The player spends stars here to upgrade the camera.

- Carver's Shelf. When every moment of a species is in the field guide, the player can order a carving of it.

- Photo board. The player's favorite photos.

- Postcard strings. When someone's story is finished, they send a postcard with a note on the back.

- Wardrobe. The jackets hang here. Every tool has its own place in the cabin.

- Packing bench. The player packs the bag here before a trip.

Planning a trip

Before a trip the player makes a few choices in the cabin. I put them there so that part of the day is already decided when she walks out of the door.

- The weather. The window shows the weather for the day. If it says cold or rain, a jacket is worth a place in the bag.

- Packing. The tools for the day are laid out on the bench by the door.

- Where to start. On the wall map the player picks the trailhead or any fire she reached on an earlier trip.

- Film. One roll is ten shots, and a new roll only comes from resting at a fire. The number at the bottom right of the viewfinder is how many shots are left.

How to play

W A S D: walk. Shift: run. C: sneak.

Left mouse: hold on rock to climb. Space: jump, or lunge up while climbing.

Right mouse: raise the camera. Left mouse: take the photo. Scroll: zoom.

R: find animals nearby. E: talk, rest, use. Tab: phone, settings, quit. I: bag.

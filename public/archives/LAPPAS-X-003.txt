FILE X-003 / Trail Adventure
A CLIMBING & WILDLIFE PHOTOGRAPHY GAME

Role: Solo developer · Indie
Date: 2026
Stack: Unity · C#
Status: PUBLIC

A first-person game about climbing and wildlife photography. An old photographer leaves you his cabin, his camera and a mountain island. Pack your bag, climb any rock face, creep up on the animals, and send your best shots to the people who live on the island.

Highlights
1. Climb anywhere steeper than 50°, on one stamina bar that hunger, cold, tiredness, injury and bag weight each eat into.
2. Photos are scored from one to three stars (close-ups, flight, eye contact, golden hour) and fill a field guide of 24 species.
3. The island, its cave and the old mine are generated in the editor, and the whole journal-style interface is drawn in code.

DESIGN INTENT / A climb that costs something

The part I care about is the climb. I want it to cost something, so that a goat photographed on a high ledge is remembered together with the way up to it.

The idea comes from my own trips. On Mount Rainier a marmot watched me from a ridge in the fog, and I only saw it because I had walked a long way up to where it lived. My photos from that trip are the first pages of Leo's Journal, the book by the armchair in the cabin. I use those trips as design rules.

- The animals live away from the path, so the player has to go up to them.

- Going up is tiring, and tiredness is what ends a trip.

- Animals leave when they see you, so a good photo takes waiting.

- What I keep from a trip is what I brought back, so the game has a cabin that keeps it.

THE CORE LOOP / A trip

Every trip starts and ends in the cabin. She packs up to four tools at the bench, reads the weather card in the window, and picks a start on the wall map, at the trailhead or at a fire she has found. I put this at home on purpose. By the time she opens the door she has already made a few decisions about the day.

On the mountain she climbs, creeps up on animals and takes photos. People at the fires ask for photos, she sends them from the phone on her wrist, and what they give back comes down on a paper parachute. A night at a fire brings a fresh roll of film. The trip ends at the summit, when she picks Go home, or when she is too worn out to go on.

- What to carry: four places in the bag, and every tool adds weight.

- Move or wait: stamina comes back fast, but hunger and tiredness build all day.

- Shoot or wait: a roll has ten shots, and a blurred photo scores half.

CLIMBING AND STAMINA / The mountain is the obstacle

The first version had set obstacles on the trails and a die roll to get past each one, and the walking between them was dead time. I replaced all of that with climbing. Any face steeper than 50 degrees can be climbed by holding the left mouse button.

Walking is free. Running costs 2.5 stamina a second, climbing up to 4, hanging still 2, a jump 5, and a lunge up the face with Space 20 at once for 1.7 metres. A second after she stops spending, the bar comes back at 20 a second. I made walking free so that players look at the mountain while they walk, not at the bar.

Five afflictions each take a coloured slice off the end of the bar: hunger, tiredness, cold, injury and the weight of the bag. The bar shrinks a slice at a time, so the player can see what took each one, and most of them can be dealt with.

- The Down Jacket cuts cold to 30 percent.

- The Flashlight is the only light in the cave. If it pushes the Down Jacket out of the bag, the snow above the mine is a much harder climb.

PHOTOGRAPHY / Keeping still and waiting

The right mouse button brings the camera up and the left button takes the shot. The score comes from what is in the frame: getting close, a bird in flight, an animal looking at the lens, a mother with her young, golden hour. The points add up to one, two or three stars. Moving shakes the camera and a blurred photo scores half, so the good photos come from keeping still.

Each species in the field guide has a row of moments, from first sight and a close-up to the things only that animal does. A new moment earns a star, and stars are the only currency, so an animal she has already photographed is still worth finding again.

The 24 species do not all react the same way. A boar charges, a squirrel runs up a tree, foxes steal snacks, and the owl only comes out between 17:30 and 6:30. If every animal ran off the same way, players would learn one trick and use it on all of them.

THE ISLAND / Sixteen people and Leo's camera

The player is a young woman with no name. Leo, an old photographer who walked this mountain for forty years, has left her his camera and a letter, and she lives in his cabin. "It's yours now," he writes. Sixteen people on the island make 38 requests between them. The eight tools come out of six of those stories, so every new ability has a person behind it.

Nell waits at the cave mouth and will not go past the daylight. She tells the player that Shepherd Bram has a flashlight. Without one, the player feels her way along the wall inside at a little over half speed. Bram's last request is the ducks on the pond, and that photo brings the Flashlight down by parachute. With a light the cave opens up: nine species, and Old Ida deep in the old mine.

Old Marten knew Leo. His last request is a ptarmigan close enough to count its feathers. When the photo works, his postcard is waiting at home: "We never did get that ptarmigan. You did."

THE CABIN AND THE JOURNAL / What the cabin keeps

After a real trip I come home with photos and a few things to remember it by, so the cabin keeps them. A finished story sends a postcard, with her photo on the front and a note and a crayon drawing on the back. A carving of a species can be ordered for the Carver's Shelf once every moment of it is in the guide, and stars buy parts for Leo's camera at the bench.

The interface follows the same idea. The HUD, the panels and the field guide look like a travel journal, with paper, washi tape, rubber stamps and sticker icons where text labels would normally go. The wrist phone is a device, so it keeps a clean screen.

NEXT / What I'd build next

At the moment only the owl keeps set hours. The next build makes the time of day part of planning a trip.

- The hedgehog comes out towards dusk and deer are easiest to find early in the morning, so requests that mention a time depend on it.

- An owl that roosts during the day, so players who never stay out after dark can still find one asleep in a tree.

- Ambient occlusion for the faceted rock and the cave, and depth of field in the viewfinder.
